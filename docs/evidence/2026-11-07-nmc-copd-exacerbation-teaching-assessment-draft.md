# COPD exacerbation teaching and assessment draft

Original draft for GM28.18, September 2024 NMC PDF page 325. Source reference nmc-2024-ref:6f8123611a283bdd6e84782c7dc1e4dc99f37471418cc12b22fde5261ebb2969. Candidate identity: COPD exacerbation management. Clinical/source review pending.

## Teaching note

Assess acute deterioration, severity and alternative explanations. GOLD identifies pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, heart failure and pneumothorax among conditions that can mimic or worsen an exacerbation. Initial bronchodilators include a short-acting beta-agonist, with or without a short-acting anticholinergic. Systemic steroids and antibiotics need indication-based decisions. Reassess the response and need for higher-level care.

## Original simulated exercises

All cases are invented. Supply the reviewed acute-care reference and an escalation pathway. Do not use this exercise as an unsupervised prescribing protocol.

| Exercise | Context | Prompt | Draft key |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | A 66-year-old with established COPD has increasing breathlessness and wheeze over three days. They remain alert and can speak in full sentences. No initial test results are supplied. | State what you assess first, which additional findings you need and how you justify initial management. | Assess severity and alternatives, select initial bronchodilator treatment, and explain further treatment decisions using the reference. |
| Changed constraint | The same patient becomes drowsy and visibly exhausted during assessment. | Explain how the new findings change urgency and the plan. | Escalate urgently for assessment of respiratory failure and supportive treatment. |
| Delayed case | A 70-year-old with COPD develops sudden breathlessness and unilateral pleuritic pain. | Explain whether an exacerbation label is sufficient and prioritize the assessment. | Investigate an alternative acute cause rather than attributing all symptoms to COPD. |

## Proposed rubric

Score four criteria independently, 0 for missing/incorrect and 1 for explicit/correct: identifies severity, considers relevant alternatives, justifies initial treatment, and plans reassessment or escalation. Record prompting and critical omissions separately. A clinician must define acceptable alternatives and the pass standard. Keep fresh cases separately versioned.
