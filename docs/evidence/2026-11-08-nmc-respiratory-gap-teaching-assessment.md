# Respiratory gap teaching and assessment drafts

Original educational authoring, 8 November 2026. These exercises address three identified components, with partial scope and unresolved canonical objective bindings. Cases are fictional. No PYQ provenance or validated pass threshold is claimed. Supply the cited reference during each exercise. This packet is not a prescribing protocol.

## PFT principles teaching

Spirometry describes forced airflow and expired volumes. Lung-volume testing addresses capacities including TLC and FRC. DLCO assesses gas transfer. These measurements answer different questions; a single result does not establish its cause. Interpret with technical quality and clinical context.

## PFT principles cases

| Exercise | Scenario and task | Draft expected evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PFT baseline | A fictional referral asks three questions: forced expiratory airflow, total lung capacity and gas transfer. Select a test category for each and explain the measured property. | Spirometry, lung-volume testing and DLCO respectively; explain each measurement. |
| PFT changed constraint | A report contains only technically acceptable spirometry. The requester asks for the measured TLC and DLCO. Explain what can be supplied from this report. | Those measurements are absent; do not invent them or treat them as equivalent to spirometry. |
| PFT delayed | An unfamiliar fictional report supplies spirometry, lung volumes and DLCO. Explain the purpose of each section before considering a diagnosis. | Distinguish airflow, volumes and gas transfer; integrate quality and context. |

## PFT principles rubric

Record each separately as absent/incorrect or explicit/correct: identifies the measurement, chooses the relevant test category, recognizes missing data, limits causal inference. Record prompting separately. Practical testing and full pattern interpretation remain outside this component.

## Systemic steroid decisions teaching

For significant COPD exacerbations, systemic corticosteroids can support recovery but carry adverse effects. Decide using severity, patient risks and a current reference; avoid automatic treatment of every symptom change. Specify reassessment. Disease breadth beyond COPD remains open.

## Systemic steroid decisions cases

| Exercise | Scenario and task | Draft expected evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steroid baseline | A fictional adult with established COPD has clinically significant acute worsening despite initial inhaled treatment. Using the supplied reference, justify a systemic-steroid decision. | Explain indication, risk assessment and reassessment. |
| Steroid changed constraint | The same fictional patient has poorly controlled diabetes. Explain what this changes in the decision and monitoring discussion. | Weigh benefit and risk; include glycaemic monitoring rather than ignoring the comorbidity. |
| Steroid delayed | A fictional patient requests another steroid course after a symptom change, but severity and alternative causes have not been assessed. Explain the next reasoning steps. | Assess before repeating treatment; identify missing information and alternatives. |

## Systemic steroid decisions rubric

Record indication reasoning, relevant risk recognition, reference-supported decision and reassessment separately. Record prompting and critical omissions. Drug, dose, route, duration and acceptable alternatives require an exact reference-supported key before prescription-level assessment.

## Antimicrobial decisions teaching

Antibiotics are not automatic for every COPD exacerbation. GOLD considers evidence such as purulence with additional symptoms, prior positive sputum culture and ventilatory support. Selection also depends on local resistance and patient factors. Document the indication and reassess.

## Antimicrobial decisions cases

| Exercise | Scenario and task | Draft expected evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Antimicrobial baseline | A fictional adult with COPD has increased dyspnoea and newly purulent sputum. Use the supplied reference to explain an antibiotic decision. | Recognize the relevant indication pattern; assess context and patient factors. |
| Antimicrobial changed constraint | A fictional adult has mild increased cough without purulence; no ventilatory support or prior positive culture is reported. Explain whether the COPD label alone establishes an antibiotic indication. | The label alone is insufficient; assess further and justify the decision. |
| Antimicrobial delayed | A fictional exacerbation requires non-invasive ventilation. A prior culture identified a resistant organism. Explain how these facts affect the antibiotic discussion. | Recognize indication and use culture history/local resistance to guide selection. |

## Antimicrobial decisions rubric

Record indication recognition, missing-information handling, resistance/patient-factor reasoning and reassessment separately. Drug selection, dose, route and duration need a current local reference and an exact answer key. These criteria do not validate prescribing competence.
